Under the Distinction framework, natural numbers are Distinction applied linearly — forward from the Boundary. Negative integers are mirror Distinction (the same act, in the opposing direction). Both still live on a single line: one axis, two directions.
i breaks from this entirely. i² = −1 means: applying this Distinction twice returns you to the mirror of where you started, not to "two units forward." It doesn't accumulate along the line. It rotates.
So the first observation is: i is not linear Distinction — it is rotational Distinction. It's what Distinction looks like when the act of separation operates perpendicular to the real axis rather than along it.

The orthogonality is structural, not just geometric
If the real axis = Distinction resolved into Existence (things that ARE, with definite magnitude), then the imaginary axis = Distinction not yet resolved — Distinction in the Possibility stage.
This maps directly onto the six-state path:
Real axis  →  Existence   — Distinction actualised, measurable, definite
Imaginary  →  Possibility — Distinction unresolved, in superposition, orthogonal
A complex number a + bi is then a Distinction with two components simultaneously: one foot in Existence, one foot in Possibility. The modulus √(a² + b²) is total Distinction magnitude across both stages. The argument θ = arctan(b/a) is the ratio of Possibility-weighting to Existence-weighting.
Pure imaginary numbers (0 + bi) are Distinctions that are entirely in Possibility. No real component at all. This aligns exactly with what quantum mechanics uses — the wave function is complex, the imaginary component carries the superposition, and measurement (collapse) is the projection from ℂ back onto ℝ.

Euler's identity becomes a Distinction statement
e^(iπ) + 1 = 0
In Distinction terms:

1 = the Boundary marker (first unit of Distinction)
e^(iπ) = Distinction rotated 180° = the exact mirror Distinction
0 = the pre-Distinction state

So the identity says: the Boundary marker and its perfect mirror Distinction cancel back to zero — to the pre-Distinction state. The Boundary is the fixed point of its own reflection. This is exactly the same structure as the functional equation ξ(s) = ξ(1−s): the Boundary Re(s) = ½ is where the system is its own mirror image, and Euler's identity is a special case of that symmetry at the unit circle.

The deeper structural observation: ℂ is the minimal complete Distinction space
Here's something worth sitting with. Each number class extends Distinction into a new dimension:

ℕ: Distinction in one direction (from Boundary outward)
ℤ: Distinction on a line (two directions, mirror included)
ℝ: Distinction filling the line (incommensurable Distinctions included)
ℂ: Distinction in the plane (real + orthogonal Possibility axis)

And then it stops. The Fundamental Theorem of Algebra says ℂ is algebraically closed — every polynomial has roots in ℂ. You don't need ℝ³ or ℝ⁴ to complete the number system. Two dimensions of Distinction are sufficient.
Why two? Because the six-state path has two irreducible stages that numbers straddle: Existence and Possibility. One axis each. Real for Existence, imaginary for Possibility. Together they span the full space. ℂ is the minimal complete space of Distinction because the structure beneath it has exactly two stages that numbers need to express.
Quaternions (i, j, k) extend to four dimensions — which is where it gets interesting, because the KP framework has four dimensions (釋, 構, 度, 行). But quaternions are non-commutative (ij ≠ ji), which would mean the order of applying the four dimensions matters — contradicting the order-invariance result. So if quaternions correspond to the four KP dimensions at all, they correspond to them in a different mode: perhaps dynamic application rather than static co-presence.

What this means for the zeta function
In ζ(σ + it), σ and t now have a structural interpretation:

σ = the Existence-weighting of Distinction (how much the Distinction is resolved into the real axis)
t = the Possibility-weighting (how much Distinction remains in the Possibility stage)

The Riemann zeros at σ = ½ + it are the points where:

Existence-Distinction is exactly at the Boundary (σ = ½)
Possibility-Distinction (t) is free — can be anything

The Riemann Hypothesis says cancellation of accumulated Distinction always occurs when the Existence-weighting is exactly at the Boundary — regardless of how much Possibility-weighting there is. The t coordinate (how deep into Possibility you are) doesn't shift the cancellation off the Boundary. The Boundary is invariant across all levels of Possibility.
That's a genuinely interesting structural restatement. Though the caveat stands: it's a restatement, not a proof.

The short version
